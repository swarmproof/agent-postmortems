# DATASHEET — agent-postmortems

*A datasheet for the corpus in the format of Gebru et al., "Datasheets for Datasets"
(Communications of the ACM 64(12), 2021, https://doi.org/10.1145/3458723).*

**Figures are as of corpus snapshot `2026-11-12` (release `2026.11.2`, export commit
`757fffc`, 180 records).** They were computed from `export/incidents.json` at that commit
and will drift as records land. The live source for headline numbers is the stats block in
[`README.md`](../README.md), regenerated by `scripts/gen_readme_stats.py` on every merge;
the live export is `https://raw.githubusercontent.com/swarmproof/agent-postmortems/main/export/incidents.json`.

## Motivation

**Why was the dataset created?** AI agents take real actions (tool calls, code
execution, payments, browsing, messaging) and fail in novel but repeating ways, and there
was no shared, structured, machine-validated record of those failures. The corpus is that
record: one schema ([`SCHEMA.md`](../SCHEMA.md)), one versioned failure taxonomy
([`TAXONOMY.md`](../TAXONOMY.md)), and sourced records that conform to both, so failures
can be compared, counted, cited, and replayed as test scenarios.

**Who created it, and who funded it?** Created and maintained by the Swarm Proof
toolkit maintainer (see [`CITATION.cff`](../CITATION.cff)) as an unfunded open-source
project. Contributors are credited in git history; the schema's `submitted_by` field is
unused on all 180 records, so authorship is not recoverable per record from the data.

## Composition

**What do the instances represent?** One instance is one publicly documented AI-agent
failure: either a realized `incident` or a demonstrated `hazard` (a researcher proof of
concept or near-miss, admitted per ADR-0005 and labeled as such). An instance is one YAML
file under `incidents/`, keyed by an immutable `incident_id` of the form `YYYY-kebab-slug`.

**How many instances are there?** 180 records: 101 incidents and 79 hazards.

**Time span.** Incident dates run from 2023-12-18 to 2026-11-12. The distribution is
heavily recent:

| Year | Records | Share |
|---|---|---|
| 2023 | 1 | 0.6% |
| 2024 | 4 | 2.2% |
| 2025 | 29 | 16.1% |
| 2026 | 146 | 81.1% |

116 records (64.4%) are dated July to October 2026 alone. This reflects when the corpus was
built and when agent deployments became common, not a measured rate of agent failures over
time; see "Known biases" below.

**Labels and fields.** Every record carries the required core (`incident_id`, `title`,
`date`, `incident_type`, `system`, `primary_failure_class`, `failure_classes`, `trigger`,
`root_cause`, `detection`, `recovery`, `prevention`, `blast_radius`, `sources`) and an
optional body; the full field reference is [`SCHEMA.md`](../SCHEMA.md). Classification is
multi-valued: `failure_classes` is an ordered causal chain whose first entry is
`primary_failure_class`.

|---|---|---|

155 records (86.1%) cite at least one `primary-research` or `vendor-disclosure` source;
18 rely only on `news` or `other` sources. The most cited publishers are The Register
(27), The Hacker News (26), BleepingComputer (19), NVD (17), Anthropic (16), Embrace The
Red (15, plus 4 under a shorter publisher string), CSO Online (13), Cloud Security Alliance
(13, plus 6 under its AI Safety Initiative), OpenAI Alignment (12) and SecurityWeek (11);
by host, github.com is the single most cited domain (47 sources). 126 of the 259 news
sources (48.6%) come from security trade press, and 48 sources are published by the
vendors Anthropic, OpenAI, Microsoft, Google, Amazon or Meta.

**Language.** The schema has no language field. All 581 source titles are in Latin
script, 391 of 581 URLs are on `.com` hosts, and only three URLs sit on non-anglophone
country domains (`heise.de`, `aepd.es`, `clauding.de`, the last an English-language
page). Record text (titles, summaries, root causes) is written in English. The corpus
should be treated as English-language and sourced from the anglophone security and AI
press.

**Archival.** 536 of 581 sources (92.3%) carry an `archive_url`; 144 records (80.0%)
have an archive URL on every source, and all 180 have one on at least one source. Missing
snapshots are backfilled by the daily publish workflow when archive.org can take them
(ADR-0004).

**Is any information confidential or sensitive?** No. Every record is built only from
public sources, and the curator standard forbids identifying harmed individuals beyond
what the cited sources already published. Export entries carry natural-language trigger
*shapes*; the export builder refuses any entry containing a fenced code block or obvious
payload markers, so no runnable exploit content is included.

**Errors, noise, redundancies.** Two records are `confidence: disputed`; neither
carries a `vendor-response` source (the 12 `vendor-response` sources sit on records whose
facts are not contested). Duplicates are prevented at curation time by URL, CVE and title
overlap, but the same campaign can legitimately appear as several records when sources
describe distinct events (`related_incidents` links them).

## Collection process

**How was the data acquired?** From public, linkable sources only. Candidates reach a
maintainer through three paths, all documented in
[`docs/CURATION-PIPELINE.md`](./CURATION-PIPELINE.md): a weekly discovery job over
security-research feeds, first-party lab and vendor disclosures, arXiv, Google News, the
NVD CVE API and the GitHub Advisory Database; a weekly completeness critic that diffs the
corpus against a curated external index of agent attacks; and direct contributions.
Records were then researched and drafted by the maintainer, by contributors, or by the
opt-in auto-draft layer, and every one passed human review before merge.

**Inclusion criteria.** From the curator standard
([`.claude/agents/incident-curator.md`](../.claude/agents/incident-curator.md)) and
[`CONTRIBUTING.md`](../CONTRIBUTING.md):

- In scope: agent or LLM systems **taking or recommending actions** (tools, code,
  messages, money, browsing). Out of scope: vendor marketing, funding news, generic "AI is
  risky" commentary, product launches.
- At least one public, linkable source; no rumors, no anonymous accounts, no speculation
  about intent. When no solid public source exists the candidate is discarded, not
  guessed.
- Prefer the strongest source type: primary research writeup, vendor disclosure or
  court/regulatory record over news coverage; corroborate with a second independent source
  where possible.
- Researcher proofs of concept and near-misses are admitted but labeled
  `incident_type: hazard`.
- `confidence` is set from sourcing: single source `reported`, multiple independent
  `corroborated`, first-party, vendor or court `confirmed`, contested facts `disputed`.
- Neutral, blameless wording: systems are named as data, not as targets; root causes are
  systemic, not personal; a lint rejects blame adjectives.

**The human gate.** Per `docs/CURATION-PIPELINE.md`, automation covers discovery,
de-duplication, drafting and validation, but **nothing enters the corpus without a human
approving a pull request**. The auto-draft layer opens PRs labeled `needs-review` and
never merges; CI being green is not editorial review.

**Over what time frame was it collected?** Record `last_updated` dates run from
2026-10-14 to 2026-11-18; the first release (`2026.10`, 63 records) was published
2026-10-22. Most of the corpus was therefore curated in a six-week window in late 2026,
including a historical backlog reaching to 2023.

## Preprocessing and validation

Records are validated, not transformed. The gates in `.github/workflows/validate.yml`
run on every pull request and on `main`:

- `scripts/validate.py`: JSON Schema conformance (`schema/incident.schema.json`,
  draft 2020-12, unknown top-level fields rejected), taxonomy conformance of every
  class/subclass, `incident_id` uniqueness and filename equality, the neutrality lint, and
  a drift check that the `SCHEMA.md` field table matches the JSON Schema's top-level
  properties; `--fixtures` runs the same gates against known-good and known-bad fixtures.
- `scripts/apply_mappings.py --check`: the `mappings` block (OWASP LLM, OWASP Agentic,
  MITRE ATLAS) is derived from `failure_classes` via `schema/framework-mappings.yaml` and
  may not be hand-edited.
- `scripts/check_links.py`: every `sources[].url` must be reachable; a pull request checks
  only the records it changes. On `main` the daily publish workflow also backfills
  `archive_url` with Wayback snapshots.
- `scripts/build_exports.py`: the three exports build and validate against
  `schema/export/*.schema.json`; a payload lint refuses scenario or seed shapes that look
  like exploit code.
- `scripts/build_site.py`: the static site builds from every record.

The exports are projections, not new data: `incidents.json` is the full validated
corpus; `scenarios.json` keeps records with a `stampede_scenario_hint`; `seeds.json` keeps
records with a cost dimension (a cost-related failure class or a dollar figure). Their
`generated_at` and `corpus_as_of` fields both equal the newest incident `date`, so a
rebuild of the same corpus is byte-identical; neither is a build time.

## Uses

**What has it been used for?** The site at https://swarmproof.github.io/agent-postmortems/
(filterable index, per-record permalinks with BibTeX, RSS) and the machine-readable exports
consumed by sibling tools: `scenarios.json` by
[stampede](https://github.com/swarmproof/stampede) for replayable chaos scenarios and
`seeds.json` by [costbomb](https://github.com/swarmproof/costbomb) for denial-of-wallet
fuzzing (ADR-0006).

**What could it be used for?** Studying agent failure modes and their causal chains;
designing evaluations and guardrail tests from observed triggers; mapping incidents to
OWASP and MITRE ATLAS controls; teaching material on blameless post-mortems; and as a
cross-reference for broader incident databases via `external_ids`.

**Known biases and what the data should not be used for.** The figures above are
measurable features of the corpus, not of the world:

- **Temporal.** 97.2% of records are dated 2025 or 2026 and 64.4% fall in July to October
  2026. The corpus does not support claims about the rate of agent failures over time.
- **Language and press.** Sources are English-language; 44.6% are news and roughly half of
  those come from security trade press. Failures that were disclosed only in other
  languages, or that never reached the security press, are under-represented.
- **Disclosure-driven.** 25.3% of sources are researcher write-ups and 15.1% are vendor
  disclosures; the lab evaluation group (28 records) is dominated by one vendor's own
  published evaluation and training-run disclosures (OpenAI is the operating vendor on 16
  of them). Vendors and researchers that publish more appear more.
  Record counts per vendor therefore measure disclosure practice at least as much as
  failure frequency and **must not be read as a safety ranking of vendors or products**.
- **System concentration.** 42.8% of records involve a coding agent, an agentic IDE or
  MCP tooling, and hazards are dominated by prompt-injection demonstrations (44 of 79).
  This tracks where security researchers currently look, not necessarily where agents
  are deployed.
- **Severity skew.** 127 of 180 records are `high` or `critical`; low-impact failures are
  rarely written up publicly, so the corpus is not a base rate for impact.
- **Coded fields are judgement calls.** `severity`, `confidence`, `autonomy_level` and the
  causal chain are assigned by a curator from the sources and reviewed, not measured.

The corpus is not suitable for training systems to produce attacks: trigger shapes are
deliberately non-executable, and records are descriptions of failures, not exploits.

## Maintenance

- **Who maintains it?** The repository maintainer listed in `CITATION.cff`; issues and
  pull requests at the repository are the contact channel.
- **Updates.** New records land through reviewed pull requests, fed by the weekly
  discovery job (Mondays), the weekly completeness critic (Wednesdays) and contributors.
  Exports, README statistics and archive snapshots refresh daily at 07:00 UTC and on every
  qualifying push to `main`. Versioned snapshots are released on a CalVer cadence.
- **Corrections and disputes.** A sourced record is never deleted (ADR-0008). If a vendor
  or other party contests the facts, the record is downgraded to
  `confidence: disputed`, their reply is added as a `vendor-response` source, and the
  dispute becomes part of the record. The process is described in
  [`DISPUTES.md`](../DISPUTES.md).
- **Versioning.** Records carry `schema_version: "1.0"` and `taxonomy_version: "1.0"`;
  the exports carry `export_schema_version: "1.0"`. Breaking changes to any of these are
  recorded in `adr/` and bump the respective version; older releases remain available
  under their tags.
- **Contributing.** See [`CONTRIBUTING.md`](../CONTRIBUTING.md) and
  [`incidents/_TEMPLATE.yaml`](../incidents/_TEMPLATE.yaml). Contributions are licensed
  under the repository licences on merge.
